Focused Nurse Assessment

  1. Verify patient identity, caregiver relationship, callback number, and current location in case urgent escalation is needed.
  2. Confirm highest temperature, route used, time measured, and whether fever-reducing medication was given.
  3. Assess vomiting frequency, last episode, ability to keep down small sips, and last urine output.
  4. Ask about abdominal pain, headache, neck stiffness, rash, breathing concerns, diarrhea, exposure to illness, and recent travel.
  5. Review age, relevant medical history, current medications, allergies, and immunization concerns from the EHR context.
If assessment reveals worsening symptoms or inability to hydrate, move to urgent disposition or physician consult workflow.

Example Disposition Options

  1. Emergency escalation: Advise emergency services or emergency department evaluation when red flags, respiratory distress, severe dehydration, altered mental status, or severe pain are reported.
  2. Same-day pediatric evaluation: Route to available pediatric clinic, urgent care, or same-day appointment when fever and vomiting continue but no emergency red flags are present.
  3. Provider callback: Send high-priority callback task when symptoms are concerning, caregiver is uncertain, or clinical protocol requires provider review.
  4. Home care with follow-up: Use only when permitted by protocol, red flags are absent, child is stable, and caregiver understands return precautions.

Caregiver Education Prompts

  1. Explain the selected disposition clearly and confirm the caregiver understands where to go or who will call back.
  2. Review return precautions using approved organizational language, including worsening condition, dehydration signs, breathing trouble, severe pain, confusion, or persistent vomiting.
  3. Confirm caregiver has transportation, access to the patient portal, and a reliable callback number.
  4. Document questions asked, caregiver responses, selected disposition, and any handoff instructions.
Do not provide medication dosing, diagnosis, or treatment instructions outside approved triage protocols.

Escalation Handoff

  1. Summarize: “Sam Law, DOB 08/08/2017, fever over 101°F with vomiting, symptom onset 6-24 hours ago.”
  2. Include selected red flags, hydration status, vomiting frequency, temperature details, and caregiver callback number.
  3. Route to the selected queue: pediatric nurse triage, same-day appointment scheduling, on-call provider, or emergency escalation support.
  4. Close the loop by documenting the final disposition and confirming caregiver instructions.
